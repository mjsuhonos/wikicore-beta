Why Wiki Core

Familiar vocabulary practice, web-native foundations

Built for libraries, archives, catalogs, and discovery systems that need a controlled vocabulary which is machine-readable, multilingual, and ready for automated workflows — without giving up human control.

hub

Anchored in Wikidata

Every concept carries a stable Wikidata QID, separating meaning from syntax and ensuring interoperability across the wider linked-data ecosystem.

account_tree

Controlled vocabulary, not ontology

Reusable SKOS concept schemes for metadata, indexing, and subject classification — practical and non-prescriptive by design.

menu_book

Paired full-text corpus

Each concept links to an English Wikipedia article, so every entry has both a human-readable text and a ready-made training document.

language

Multilingual

Native labels in seven languages, with editions for English, Catalan, German, Spanish, Finnish, French, and Italian.

extension

Modular subject groups

61 reusable vocabularies — core concepts, class groups, and occupation groups — that intentionally overlap rather than force a single taxonomy.

volunteer_activism

Humans in control

Automation handles extraction, normalization, and linking; people define concepts, scope, and the relationships that make discovery meaningful.

Two sources, one system

Wikidata + Wikipedia

Wiki Core brings stable identifiers and article text together as reusable SKOS vocabularies and linked JSONL text corpora — without introducing a separate identifier system.

dns

Wikidata

Stable identifiers, multilingual labels, and structured relationships — subclass of, part of, instance of, occupation — provide the vocabulary's backbone.

article

Wikipedia

Article text and the links between concepts, processed into clean JSONL documents keyed by QID, with article links resolved back to Wikidata entities.

Two guarantees: every Wiki Core entry links directly to an English Wikipedia article for end users — and every entry therefore has an English training document, ready for automated subject indexing and model training.
Three layers

Core, classes, and occupations

The vocabulary is organized into modular layers. Groups are convenience views over Wikidata — they may overlap, by design.

widgetsCore

104K concepts

Pure concepts derived from Wikidata subclass-of and part-of relationships — the conceptual backbone of the vocabulary.

categoryClasses

41 groups · 3.5M documents

Practical groupings of things — science, music, film, geography, organizations — each expanded through instance-of relationships.

groupsOccupations

19 groups · 2.5M documents

Collections of people derived from occupation relationships — engineers, physicians, artists, athletes, politicians.

Built for automated indexing

From public dumps to subject indexing

A reproducible Makefile pipeline turns Wikidata and Wikipedia dumps into ready-to-use vocabularies, corpora, and trained indexing projects.

1

filter_altExtract

Wikidata properties (P31, P279, P361, P106) and sitelinks; Wikipedia XML converted to clean JSONL by wiki2jsonl.py.

2

constructionBuild

SKOS vocabularies (.nt / .ttl.gz) and full-text corpora, split 80/10/10 into train, eval, and test sets.

3

rocket_launchIndex

Load into Annif: train, evaluate, and run automated subject indexing with ready-made project configurations.

One command — make all — reproduces the full release from source dumps.

Open and community-shaped

Inspectable, openly licensed, open to collaboration

Wiki Core is deliberately not a product of any single institution — it is built in the open, and its direction is shaped in public.

public

Open and inspectable

The entire build pipeline and all generated data live in the repository. Licensed under GPLv3 — free to use, adapt, and redistribute, with the build reproducible end to end.

groups

Community-shaped governance

Significant changes to vocabulary structure, identifier policy, or philosophy are discussed openly in GitHub Issues before implementation. Contributions, critique, and questions are welcome.
